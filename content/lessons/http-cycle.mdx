You just shipped your first app. Someone types your URL and hits Enter.

What happens in the next 100 milliseconds?

That conversation — the one between your visitor's browser and your server — runs on a protocol called **HTTP** (HyperText Transfer Protocol). It's the language the whole web speaks.

## The Two-Part Conversation

Every HTTP interaction is exactly two messages: a **request** from the browser, and a **response** from the server. That's it. No session, no persistent connection — just ask and answer, over and over.

Step 1: Your server starts up, waiting. Step 2: Someone's browser sends a request — "Give me the page at `/`." Step 3: Your server sends back HTML and says "200 OK — here it is."

## What's Inside a Request

Every request has three things:

- **Method** — what action to take. `GET` means "give me data." `POST` means "here's some data, do something with it." `DELETE` means "remove this."
- **URL** — where to send the request, like `/api/todos` or `/users/42`
- **Headers** — metadata attached to the request, like what format the browser accepts (`Accept: application/json`)

When you submit a form or click "Save," your app sends a `POST` request. When you load a page, it's a `GET`.

## What's Inside a Response

The server's reply always starts with a **status code** — a three-digit number that tells the browser what happened:

| Code | Meaning |
|------|---------|
| 200 | OK — everything worked |
| 201 | Created — a new thing was made |
| 301 | Moved — the URL changed permanently |
| 400 | Bad Request — the browser sent something garbled |
| 401 | Unauthorized — you need to be logged in |
| 404 | Not Found — nothing exists at that URL |
| 500 | Server Error — something broke on your end |

When your app crashes and you see a blank screen, somewhere there's a `500` response. When you mistype a URL, there's a `404`.

## Why This Matters to You

Every single feature in your vibe-coded app is HTTP under the hood:

- **Loading a page** → `GET /`
- **Fetching your todos** → `GET /api/todos`
- **Saving a new item** → `POST /api/items`
- **Deleting a record** → `DELETE /api/items/42`

When something isn't working, the HTTP cycle is the first place to look. Open your browser's DevTools, click the Network tab, and you can see every single request and response your app is making. Slow requests, failed requests, wrong data — it's all there.
