Your app handles 10 users fine. Then it goes viral. Something breaks. What is it?

## The Usual Suspects

When traffic spikes, systems fail in a predictable order:

1. **Database** — almost always the first bottleneck. Queries that take 5ms at 10 users take 500ms at 1,000 concurrent users. Connection pools fill up. Queries queue.

2. **Server memory** — each request consumes RAM. With 1,000 concurrent requests, your server might run out of memory and crash.

3. **Server CPU** — if your code does heavy computation per request (image processing, complex queries, rendering), CPU maxes out.

4. **External APIs** — rate limits from third-party services (payment processors, email providers) can queue or fail requests.

## Reading the Signs

| Symptom | Likely cause |
|---------|-------------|
| Slow responses, increasing over time | Database overload |
| Sudden crashes, restarts | Out of memory |
| 429 errors | Rate limiting |
| Timeouts on specific endpoints | Slow queries or external calls |

## The Good News

These failure modes are well-understood. Every scaling technique — caching, indexing, load balancing — addresses one of them specifically. Knowing what breaks first tells you what to fix first.
